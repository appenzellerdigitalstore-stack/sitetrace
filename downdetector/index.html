About this service status checker

When something stops working, the first question is "is it me, or is it them?" This page answers that for 50+ popular services in real time. Data comes straight from each service's official status page (statuspage.io) so you can see if they have declared an outage, plus a 24-hour timeline of incidents. For services we don't track, the search bar deep-links to Downdetector's community reports.

Frequently asked questions

How do I check if a website is down?

Type the service name in the search bar above. If we track it, you will see its current status and any recent incidents inline. If we don't, we link you to Downdetector's community reports for that service, which is the de-facto source for "is it just me or is everyone affected".

What is the difference between "down for me" and "down for everyone"?

"Down for me" means your connection cannot reach the service, but the service itself is fine for everyone else — usually a problem with your network, ISP, DNS, or VPN. "Down for everyone" means the service has declared an outage or degradation on its official status page, affecting all (or many) users. The two require very different responses.

Where does this data come from?

Status data comes from each service's official status page, mostly hosted on Atlassian Statuspage. We poll the public summary endpoint and the incident feed. 24-hour incident timelines are derived from the incident history. We do not guess or infer outages from user reports — only declared ones.

How often is the status updated?

When you load the page, every service is fetched in parallel (with a small concurrency cap to respect the public API). The result you see is at most a few seconds old. There is no background polling — every refresh is fresh.

What does "monitored" mean on a service card?

Some services do not publish a public status page. For those, the card shows "monitored" with a link to the official site. We cannot tell you in real time whether they are down, but the link lets you check directly or jump to Downdetector for community reports.
