How this ping test works

A ping test measures the round-trip time it takes for a small packet of data to travel from your device to a target server and back. It is the most direct way to feel your connection's latency: lower numbers mean a snappier connection for gaming, video calls, and real-time apps. This tool pings nine major global servers every few seconds so you can see how your latency varies and which region is closest to you.

Frequently asked questions

What is a ping test?

A ping test sends a small network packet (an ICMP echo request) from your device to a target server and measures how long it takes to get a reply. The result, in milliseconds, is called latency. Lower latency means the connection feels faster, especially for real-time applications like gaming and video calls.

What is a good ping?

Under 20 ms is excellent (typical for local servers and fiber connections). 20-50 ms is good (typical for nearby regional servers). 50-100 ms is average and may show occasional lag. Above 100 ms will feel sluggish for real-time apps. The pings you see here are from your location to global servers, so even fast connections show higher numbers for far-away targets.

What is the difference between ping and latency?

They are essentially the same thing measured differently. Ping is the action of sending the test packet; latency is the result. The round-trip time (RTT) you see in a ping test is a measure of latency. "Ping" is also commonly used as a verb: "ping me when you're done".

Why is my ping so high?

Common causes include physical distance to the target server, congested network paths between you and that server, a slow Wi-Fi connection, background traffic on your network (streaming, downloads, other devices), or a VPN adding extra hops. If only some servers show high ping, the issue is the path; if all of them do, the issue is your local connection.

Can I ping a website?

Yes, technically. Most websites respond to ICMP pings, but many do not (cloud providers like Cloudflare, AWS, and Google block pings by default to reduce noise). This tool pings the major services that do respond so you get a meaningful number. For services that block ping, the row will show "unreachable" — that is normal and not a sign of a problem.
